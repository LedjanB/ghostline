# Ghostline: The Story of Vigil

2026-11-06 · Ledjan Bajrami
Ghostline happens in one night, in one town called Vigil, between 3:33 and sunrise. Milo is the only one you play. Everyone else is someone you meet.

## What changed

- **One playable character: Milo.** The character select goes away. The other 7 characters become people in the story.
- **Powers come from people, not from a shop.** Each world's resident helps Milo and gives him one power. He keeps it for the rest of the game. By world 8 he has 7 powers plus Boo's lantern.
- **Perks become powers.** The old perks are folded into what each ally gives (Ivy keeps THE STRETCH back, Lila's rope is a double jump, and so on).
- **Candles stay the currency**, spent between runs on upgrades for Milo's powers (Beta).
- **Not here:** code, art specs, numbers. This doc is the story and the rules. Tuning comes in the boss prototype.

## Who is on screen

Most of the time only Milo is on screen. THE STRETCH is heard, not seen, until you fail. Boo only comes when Milo needs him.

### THE STRETCH: heard, not seen

- **Normally:** off screen, behind you. You know where it is from sound (footsteps, the click of keys in its joints, breathing, at the end the hum) and from the meter's face icon, which still fills toward red.
- **What you see when it's close:** its shadow. A long thin shadow stretches in from the left edge across the floor and walls, longer the closer it is. Never the body.
- **It shows itself only when you fail:** a stumble = its arm lunges in from the left edge, grabs at Milo, and pulls back out. Caught = the grab scene, its full body and face. That is the only time you see the grin.
- **Its tricks per world** become shadow and sound tells: the shadow drops from the ceiling, the shadow comes up from a drawer, and so on. Miss the answer and the real thing comes out.
- **Why this is better:** being chased by something you can't see is scarier, the screen is calmer on a phone, and the grab scene hits much harder when it's the first time you see it up close.

### Boo: comes when he's needed

- **Normally:** not on screen. Boo's lantern hangs from Milo's hand and glows when Boo is near. His lines come as short text, with no ghost on screen.
- **He appears for:** the flights (chasms, the Boo orb, set pieces), the save on first runs, the ally scenes, the gates between worlds, and the ending.
- **In boss fights:** he waits at the edge of the arena and shows up once per fight to carry Milo out of one big attack.
- **His lines** become rarer and mean more: he speaks when he arrives, when he leaves, and when something matters.

## Powers and controls

The game is played on phones, PCs and gamepads, so there are only 4 actions on every device, and they never change: move, jump, dodge, light. Attacks are automatic, like the runner's weapons. Each new power upgrades one of the 4 actions instead of adding a button.
Each boss comes at the end of its world, after you've met that world's ally, so Milo always fights with every power he has so far, the new one included. The fight is built to need the new power.

| Action | Phone | Keyboard | Gamepad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move | left thumb: hold and slide | A D / arrows | stick or d-pad |
| Jump (hold = higher) | right thumb: tap | W / Up / Space | A |
| Dodge | right thumb: swipe (sideways = that way) | Shift / S / Down | B |
| Light | LIGHT button, only when charged | E | X |

## The town: Vigil

Vigil is a small town with one rule nobody remembers making: **somebody always keeps a light on.** It is not on any map. It has a river, a hill, a hospital, a park, a cemetery, a closed carnival, and old tunnels underneath. It feels like a town you drove through once at night and can't name.

### What the town looks like

- Every house has a lamp in the front window. People leave it on out of habit. Ask why and they say "my grandmother did it."
- The streetlights are old gas lamps, wired for electricity later. Some still have the little brass hook where the lamplighter hung his ladder.
- The town clock on the hospital tower has been stuck at 3:33 for as long as anyone can remember. People stopped noticing.
- Shops close early. Nobody walks after midnight. Nobody says why.

### The history, in order

1. **The well.** The first people who came to the valley found a stone well on the hill with no bottom. You could drop a lantern in and watch it fall until it was a dot. It never hit anything.
2. **The dark that comes up.** On nights with no moon, the dark came up out of the well. Not smoke. Just less light. Dogs would not go near it. A shepherd's lamp went out by itself and he was not found.
3. **The keepers.** The founders learned one thing: the dark does not come where a light is burning. So they built the town around the well, put a lamp on every corner and paid a **night keeper** to walk the streets and keep them lit. The first keeper was a man named **Elias Wick**.
4. **The one minute.** Every night at 3:33 the keepers changed shifts at the well. One minute where nobody was watching. For a hundred years that minute was enough to be careful.
5. **The night Wick didn't come back.** One night, at 3:33, Elias Wick went down the well with his lamp. Nobody knows why. His lamp was still burning when it fell out of sight. The town got a new keeper and stopped talking about it.
6. **St. Mercy.** Much later the town covered the well and built a hospital on top of it. The well is still there, under the morgue, behind a door in the boiler room. The hospital took over the job without knowing it: its lights were always on, all night, every night.
7. **Forgetting.** People stopped believing. The keeper job became the night janitor job. The lamps became habit. The carnival came in 1931, opened on a moonless night, and closed after the first weekend. Nobody wrote down what happened.
8. **Tonight.** St. Mercy closes tomorrow. Its last night has one patient (Milo), one nurse (Ivy) and one night janitor (Boo). At 3:33 the power is cut on schedule.

### Why the dark came

The dark did not come. It was always there, under the hill, waiting for a minute with no light. Tonight it got one. The hospital's power went off at 3:33, the generator didn't start, and the only person who knew why that mattered died on the stairs trying to start it. The door in the boiler room opened. The dark started coming up, one room at a time, and it is going to fill the whole town before sunrise.
The player never reads this history in one place. It comes in pieces: journal pages, what allies say, what landmarks show.

## Boo and THE STRETCH

Boo is the night janitor who died tonight. THE STRETCH is the dark wearing his shadow. That is the game's one twist, and no one in the game ever says it out loud.

### Boo

- **Real name:** Tobias Boone. Night janitor at St. Mercy for 31 years. The kids on the children's ward called him Boo because he used to hide behind the curtains and say it. He thought that was funny every single time.
- **What he knew:** his grandmother was the last real night keeper. She told him the rule: never let every light go out at once. He thought it was a story. He kept the rule anyway, because it cost nothing.
- **How he died:** at 3:31 he saw the power-off notice on the board. He ran down to the boiler room to start the old generator by hand. His heart gave out on the stairs at 3:33. His lantern rolled down the steps and went out.
- **What he is now:** still on shift. He can't clock out until the lights are back on. He doesn't know he's dead for the first few minutes, and when he finds out he doesn't make a speech about it.
- **Look:** a small round ghost with the janitor cap he died in, his key ring, and a lantern that only lights when he's carrying Milo. **He casts no shadow.** Nobody points this out.
- **Voice:** tired, kind, a little funny the way old men are funny. He talks to Milo like a night shift coworker. "Okay. Okay. We go left."

### THE STRETCH

- **What the game shows:** tall, thin, too many joints, long arms, a white face with a wide grin. It came up from the boiler room at 3:33. It chases Milo all night.
- **What it really is:** when Boo fell on the stairs, his lantern was the last light in the building. As it rolled and went out, his shadow was thrown long and thin down the stairwell, bent over every step. In that moment the dark came up and took the shadow. THE STRETCH is the dark, wearing that shape. That is why it is so tall, why it bends at the wrong places (the steps), and why it smiles: Boo was smiling, it was a joke on his last night.
- **What it wants:** nothing you'd understand. It is hungry for light. It does not hate Milo. It just goes where the light is, and Milo is carrying the last of it.

### The clues (planted, never explained)

1. Boo has no shadow. Milo does.
2. THE STRETCH's joints click like a key ring. Boo's keys make the same sound.
3. At the near stage it hums. The tune is the one Boo whistles in the ward.
4. Boo can never look at it. Every time it's close, he turns away.
5. It never chases Boo. Only Milo.
6. Journal page 1 shows Boo on the stairs with his lantern. In the drawing his shadow is missing too.
7. In the ending, when the light cracks THE STRETCH, a shadow slides back across the floor and joins Boo's feet. Then he clocks out. Nobody comments.

The player is allowed to miss all of it and still enjoy the game. The ones who catch it should get a cold feeling, not a reveal screen.

## Milo

Milo is 16, in St. Mercy for one night after falling off a roof he shouldn't have been on. He is the last patient on the last night, and he is not the hero type.
- **Why he's there:** a concussion check. Overnight observation. His mom works nights in another town and comes in the morning. The ward was emptied for the closing; they kept one bed open for him.
- **His link to Boo:** when Milo was 8 he spent a long winter on the children's ward. The night janitor used to say "Boo" from behind the curtain and leave a candy on the tray. Milo forgot him. Boo didn't forget Milo. Milo only remembers in chapter 8.
- **Look:** hospital gown over jeans (he got half dressed to leave early), one sneaker untied, a wristband with his name, a bandage on his forehead. Hoodie over the gown after the ward.
- **Personality:** quiet, dry, scared and trying not to show it. He makes small jokes when he's scared, and they get shorter as the night goes on. He does not make speeches.
- **Voice:** "Okay." "No. No no no." "Is that thing smiling?" "Boo. Boo, which way."
- **What he carries:** Boo's lantern from chapter 1 (his basic attack in boss fights). One power from each ally after that.

### 1. Ward 4, St. Mercy

- **What happened:** 3:33. Every light on the ward went off at once. The patients were moved out last week. The beds are still made.
- **New rule, light pools:** the few working ceiling lamps make pools of light on the floor. Under a lamp, THE STRETCH can't gain on you. In the dark between them it gains fast. Lamps flicker before they die, so you learn to read them.
- **Ally, Nurse Ivy:** the night nurse, alive, hiding in the nurse station with a penlight and a cold coffee. She has been here 9 years and has never once seen Boo smile at anything but the kids. **Power, Penlight:** hold attack to charge a beam; it stuns bosses and pushes THE STRETCH back a step.
- **Boss, The Night Matron:** Sister Agathe, head nurse of Ward 4 a long time ago. She still does her rounds, and her rule is lights out. She rolls on a frame of IV poles, switches the arena's lamps off one by one, and pulls the bed curtains along their rails in long sweeps (jump them). She stops at each bed to check it, and that's when you hit her. Beaten, she sits on the last bed and folds her hands.
- **Landmark:** the clock over the nurse station, stopped at 3:33, with Boo's cap on the counter under it.

### 2. The Cold Room

- **What happened:** the morgue under the hospital, next to the boiler room. The door to the well is down here. This is where the dark came in first.
- **New rule, frost floor:** the floor is iced. Slides go twice as far. Landings skid. Your breath shows in the air, and when the cooling hum stops, something in a drawer moves.
- **Ally, Ansel:** the man on slab 9. Dead 40 years, nobody ever claimed him, so the hospital kept him. He sits up under his sheet, points the way and lies back down. **Power, The Sheet:** a short vanish. You pass through one attack, and THE STRETCH loses you for a second.
- **Boss, The Coroner:** Doctor Lisle, who filed every death in Vigil. He throws toe tags; a tag that hits you "files" you (slower for 2 s). He pulls drawers out of the wall as pushers and platforms. He stops to write on his clipboard; hit him then.
- **Landmark:** drawer 9, pulled out, empty, the sheet folded neatly, still a little warm.

### 3. Lamplight Park

- **What happened:** the town park, out the hospital's back gate. It was lit with old lamps all the way through. In 1962 there was a power cut during the evening fair and a girl went missing. The trees grew over the lamps after that.
- **New rule, two paths:** the path forks. One runs high, through the branches; one runs low, on the roots. Only one has a lamp lit ahead. Pick it.
- **Ally, Lila:** the girl who went missing in 1962. Eight years old, red raincoat, a skipping rope. She thinks she's late for dinner. She doesn't know what year it is and Milo doesn't tell her. **Power, Skipping rope:** a double jump.
- **Boss, The Park Keeper:** the groundskeeper who was told to find Lila that night and never did. He still sweeps the paths. Tall, a lamppost for a spine, a broom for one arm. Low broom sweeps (jump), rake slams (dodge), he shakes the lamps and the glass falls. Beaten, he sees Lila behind you and stops.
- **Landmark:** an oak that swallowed a lamppost. The lamp is still lit, inside the trunk, shining out through the bark.

### 4. Crowsrest

- **What happened:** the cemetery on the hill road. 400 graves. 399 have someone in them. The 400th was dug for Elias Wick, the keeper who went down the well. It has been open ever since.
- **New rule, the crows:** noise wakes the crows. Hard landings, misses and weapon shots startle them, and when they rise THE STRETCH hears it and gets closer. Clean running keeps them asleep.
- **Ally, Dex:** 17, the gravedigger's son. Alive. He sleeps in the tool shed most nights because it's quieter than home. He has a shovel and opinions about ghosts, all of them wrong. **Power, Shovel:** a ground slam from the air. Breaks things, stuns bosses.
- **Boss, The Widow of Grave 400:** Agnes Wick, still waiting for her husband to come home. Black veil, a crow on each shoulder. Crows dive in lines; she rings the grave bell and the ground jumps (jump the shockwave); hands come up from the graves near her. Beaten, she sits on the edge of the open grave and keeps waiting.
- **Landmark:** grave 400, open, a bell on a pole beside it, fresh flowers every night. Nobody knows who brings them.

### 5. Old Vigil

- **What happened:** the tunnels under the town aren't sewers. They're the first Vigil, the one built around the well before the hill was raised. Streets, doorways, a corner lamp. The new town was built on its roof.
- **New rule, the tide:** every 20 seconds or so the water rises. You hear it first. The floor goes under and you run on the high pipes and ledges until it drops.
- **Ally, Mott:** a rat-catcher who worked the tunnels 100 years ago. Missing teeth, a hook pole with a lantern on the end, laughs at his own jokes. He calls Milo "young sir". **Power, Hook pole:** a long vault. Clears wide gaps and lets you hang from a ledge for a moment when the tide comes up.
- **Boss, Old Swallow:** something huge and pale that lives in the outflow. It swallowed old Vigil's corner lamp long ago and the lamp still glows in its throat. It lunges from the water, pulls you toward the edge (run against it), spits up stones. Hit the glow when it opens wide. It never speaks.
- **Landmark:** a front door set into the tunnel wall, with a lamp in its window, still burning. Someone still keeps the rule down here.

### 6. The Bone Halls

- **What happened:** under the church at the bottom of the hill. The old keepers were buried here, with their lamps, and their names were cut into the wall. Every keeper of Vigil is on the list.
- **New rule, echo:** every sound comes back a second later, and so does every trap. Each one comes twice: once real, once a pale copy right behind it. The copy hurts just the same.
- **Ally, Nub:** a three-legged dog. The stray Boo fed behind the hospital every night for 6 years. Nub came looking for him and got lost down here. He knows Milo smells like Boo's lantern. **Power, Fetch:** Nub runs ahead and Milo follows him in a fast dash. Use it to dodge, or to close the gap on a boss.
- **Boss, The List-Keeper:** Brother Hesk, who cuts the names into the wall. He has started a new one. Chalk lines across the floor (hop them), skulls in the pillars turn and stare in beams (slide under), letters fall from the wall where he carves. Hit him when he steps back to read what he wrote.
- **Landmark:** the Wall of Names. The last name is Tobias Boone, freshly cut. Under it, in chalk, the letters M, I, L.

### 7. The Midnight Fair

- **What happened:** the carnival came to Vigil in 1931, opened on a night with no moon and closed after one weekend. People went into the ride called THE OTHER SIDE and the ride came back empty. The fair stayed on the flats by the river. The music never stopped.
- **New rule, the beat:** everything here moves on the music. Traps swing, drop and spin on the beat. Listen and you can run it with your eyes half shut.
- **Ally, Oskar:** a stilt man from the fair, 3 meters tall on his stilts, very polite, very sad. He was the one who walked the crowd home at night. **Power, Long step:** a glide. Jumps carry further and you fall slowly, and Boo's flights last longer.
- **Boss, Pell the Barker** (villain, see below): in the big top. Carousel horses charge on the beat, his tickets cut like blades, the loudspeakers blast in rings. He stops to tip his hat to the crowd that isn't there; hit him then.
- **Landmark:** the entrance to THE OTHER SIDE ride. A swan boat waits in black water. The sign says ONE TICKET.

### 8. The Bottom

- **What happened:** the bottom of the well. Where Elias Wick went. Where the dark comes from. Look up and St. Mercy hangs upside down above you, its windows like stars.
- **New rule, upside down:** gravity flips at the markers, floor to ceiling and back. Every rule from the 7 worlds comes back, one at a time, faster.
- **Ally, Tobias:** Boo, alive, the way he was. A memory. He's mopping a hallway that loops. He doesn't know Milo yet; he says "Visiting hours are over, kid" and gives him a candy. That's when Milo remembers him. **Power, Clock Out:** the last light. Fills Boo's lantern all the way for the final fight and the ending.
- **Boss, The Lamplighter** (villain, see below): at the door. Then the ending as built: the light, the journal, THE STRETCH cracks, the ward lights up, Boo clocks out.
- **Landmark:** St. Mercy, upside down, overhead. The window of Ward 4 is the only one lit.

## Villains and allies

Three villains: THE STRETCH chases you, The Lamplighter puts the lights out, Pell sells the way across. The bosses in worlds 1 to 6 are not villains; they are people the dark got, still doing their old jobs.

### THE STRETCH

- **Look:** tall, thin, joints in the wrong places, long arms, a white face with a grin. In the run you only see its shadow. The body and the face only when you stumble (an arm) or get caught (all of it).
- **Voice:** it never speaks. Its sounds are its voice: the click of keys in its joints, and at the near stage, a hum. Boo's tune, a little off.

### The Lamplighter

- **Who:** Elias Wick, Vigil's first night keeper, who went down the well at 3:33 and never came back. He found the bottom, and the dark, and he stopped fighting it.
- **Look:** a coat to the floor, a wide hat with no face under the brim, only a thin line of smoke. A brass snuffer on a long pole. A ladder on his back. He is the same height as THE STRETCH. Nobody comments.
- **What he does in the run:** from world 3 on he appears on the rooftops or the far ledges, reaches out with the snuffer and puts out one lamp. Each time he does, the world starts to shift (see Reality Shift).
- **What he wants:** for everyone to rest. He thinks the light is the cruel part.
- **Voice:** "Shh. It's late. Let it be dark now."

### Pell the Barker

- **Who:** Augustus Pell, who ran the Midnight Fair in 1931. He sold tickets to THE OTHER SIDE ride, and the people who rode it never came back out. He works for the dark the way a salesman works for a company: he likes the job.
- **Look:** a striped coat two sizes too big, a dented top hat, a body that leans to one side, a grin wider than his face. Gloves with too many fingers.
- **Where he shows up:** his voice comes over old loudspeakers from world 5 on, in the tunnels, in the Bone Halls, then for real in world 7.
- **Voice:** "Step right up, son. One ticket, one ride. Everybody rides."

## Reality Shift

A Reality Shift is what the world looks like if the lights never come back. The Lamplighter causes it, the dark from the well fills it, and Milo has to run through it until he reaches the next lit lamp.

### In the story

- The door at the bottom of the well is open. Tonight the dark is filling Vigil from below, and the deeper Milo goes, the closer the Other Side is to the surface.
- When The Lamplighter snuffs a lamp, that part of town falls through. For a stretch of the run, Milo is in the Other Side's copy of it: the same place, a hundred years after the dark won.
- In the shifted world you see the people who were lost there, as pale shapes doing what they were doing that night. The patients in their beds on Ward 4. The 1962 fair crowd in the park. The 1931 riders lined up at the swan boat. They don't look at Milo. Mostly.

### How it plays

- **Trigger:** from world 3 on, The Lamplighter appears and snuffs a lamp. A hush (the existing scare beat), then the colors drain and the world turns. Never during a boss fight or a flight set piece.
- **Way out:** reach the next lit lamp, about 15 to 20 s ahead. Boo's lantern shows the direction.
- **What changes:** the world's own rule turns against you (table), THE STRETCH's shadow stays at full length the whole time (one stumble and it's on you), and the shifted world has its own pickups: shift candles and pages you can only find there.
- **In world 8 there is no way back.** The Bottom is already the Other Side. The whole world is shifted.

| World | Its rule | Shifted |
|---|---|---|
| Ward 4 | Light pools keep THE STRETCH back | The pools are the dangerous part: things wait in the light |
| The Cold Room | Frost floor, long slides | The frost is on the ceiling and drips; jumps are short and heavy |
| Lamplight Park | Two paths, take the lit one | The unlit path is the safe one |
| Crowsrest | Noise wakes the crows | The crows are already up; only silence keeps them off you |
| Old Vigil | The tide rises, go high | The tide falls and the high pipes come down at you |
| The Bone Halls | Every trap comes twice | The copy comes first |
| The Midnight Fair | Everything moves on the beat | The music skips; the beat lies |

In the story, Reality Shift is also how the player learns the history: each shifted world shows one thing that happened there, without words.

## The voice

In-game text is short, plain and specific: a normal detail, then one wrong thing. The fun comes from people being people, never from the game winking at you.

### Rules

1. One short line. Simple words. All caps, as now.
2. Normal first, then wrong. "400 GRAVES. ONE IS OPEN."
3. Specific numbers and times: 3:33, 400, 1962, slab 9. Never "ancient", "cursed", "evil".
4. Never explain. No line says what THE STRETCH is, why the dark came, or what a rule is for.
5. No jokes from the game itself. Characters can be dry or a little funny (Boo is tired, Dex is wrong about everything). The game narrator never is.
6. Repetition and counting are allowed. "THE FIRST TIME." "THE SECOND TIME."
7. Some moments get no text at all. Scares, the Shift, the ending close-up.
8. Never talk to the player about being a player.

### The 12 journal pages, new

They add up to the ending as built: Boo kept the lights on, the night he died they went out, a door opened.
1. BOO WAS THE NIGHT JANITOR / AT ST. MERCY. 31 YEARS. / HE NEVER CLOCKED OUT.
2. HIS GRANDMOTHER TOLD HIM: / NEVER LET EVERY LIGHT / GO OUT AT ONCE.
3. THE COLD ROOM HUMS ALL NIGHT. / AT 3:33 IT STOPS. / FOR ONE MINUTE.
4. LAMPLIGHT PARK, 1962. / THE POWER WENT OUT / DURING THE FAIR.
5. CROWSREST HAS 400 GRAVES. / 399 ARE FULL.
6. SOMEONE BRINGS FLOWERS / TO GRAVE 400. / EVERY NIGHT.
7. THERE IS A TOWN / UNDER THE TOWN. / ITS LAMPS ARE STILL LIT.
8. THE BONE HALLS KEEP A LIST. / EVERY KEEPER OF VIGIL. / THE LAST NAME IS NEW.
9. THE FAIR CLOSED IN 1931. / THE RIDE CAME BACK EMPTY. / THE MUSIC DIDN'T STOP.
10. THE FIRST KEEPER / WENT DOWN THE WELL. / HIS LAMP WAS STILL LIT.
11. THE DARK ISN'T EVIL. / IT GOES WHERE THE LIGHT IS. / YOU ARE CARRYING IT.
12. TAKE THE LIGHT TO THE BOTTOM / AND THE LIGHTS COME BACK ON. / PROBABLY.
